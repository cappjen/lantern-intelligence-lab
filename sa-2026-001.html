Strategic Assessment · SA-2026-001

The Digital Evolution of Human Trafficking Recruitment

How technology is reshaping recruitment, control, and detection.

Research in progress Public project page 2026

01

Project objective

Understand a changing recruitment environment.

This assessment examines how digital platforms, online communication, and emerging technologies may alter the ways traffickers identify, approach, recruit, and control vulnerable people—and how those same technologies may support earlier detection and prevention.

The project will distinguish documented evidence from analytical judgments, identify gaps in the public record, and avoid implying that any single platform or technology causes trafficking.

Research questions

Questions guiding the assessment

  1. 01How are digital platforms changing the pathways through which recruitment and grooming occur?
  2. 02Which technologies are used to facilitate coercion, surveillance, isolation, or financial control?
  3. 03How do patterns differ across sex trafficking and labor trafficking contexts?
  4. 04What observable indicators may help platforms, service providers, researchers, and investigators detect risk?
  5. 05Where does available evidence remain incomplete, inconsistent, or vulnerable to overstatement?

Scope

Focused, public-source inquiry

In scope

Recruitment and control

Technology-enabled contact, grooming, deception, coercion, monitoring, payments, and movement across documented trafficking contexts.

Geography

United States focus

U.S.-relevant cases, research, and policy sources, with international evidence included when it clarifies a technology or recruitment pattern.

Evidence window

Contemporary developments

Recent public records and scholarship, interpreted alongside earlier foundational research where needed to establish change over time.

Boundaries

No private-person investigations

The assessment will not identify victims unnecessarily, conduct investigations into private individuals, or treat allegations as established facts.

Planned report structure

From context to implications

01

Key judgments

The assessment’s principal findings, confidence levels, and intelligence gaps.

02

Digital recruitment environment

How online discovery, communication, identity, and trust affect recruitment pathways.

03

Technology-enabled control

Documented uses of surveillance, threats, financial tools, and platform features.

04

Typologies and case patterns

Comparative patterns across public cases without generalizing beyond the evidence.

05

Detection and prevention

Indicators, intervention opportunities, and limits of technology-based detection.

06

Outlook and research gaps

Emerging issues, unresolved questions, and priorities for continued collection.

Evidence approach

Traceable sources, careful claims.

The project will synthesize publicly available court records, government publications, peer-reviewed research, credible institutional reports, platform transparency material, and carefully evaluated reporting.

  • Corroborate material claims across independent sources where possible.
  • Separate facts, allegations, and analytical judgments.
  • Record source provenance and apply consistent coding rules.
  • State confidence, limitations, and missing evidence explicitly.
  • Minimize victim-identifying and unnecessarily harmful detail.
